RoadVector

Overview

Pick a road on the globe, drop into the saddle and follow a race course built from connected OpenStreetMap roads. Your tires meet the actual triangles used for the road and terrain. Ride beyond the starting area and the terrain keeps streaming under the bike as the race and local traffic fall behind.

Architecture

The bike has a persistent globe position, while its six-body physics solver works in a local frame measured in metres. Rebasing that frame keeps the solver near its origin as the rider travels. The streamer publishes complete collision surfaces to the solver. When required tiles are missing, the physics clock waits so the tires cannot fall through a temporary hole. Road contacts retain segment identifiers, tying the race and the tire model to the same source network. Surface streaming · Road collider · Ride runtime

Globe and riding surface

RoadVector is a location-based motorcycle racing demo built from reusable PeerCompute geospatial, particle-rigid, traffic, and WebXR modules. It loads a bounded OpenStreetMap-derived seed road scene from services/geodata, then streams AWS Terrarium elevation chunks continuously around a WGS84/ECEF globe position. The seed graph supplies a deterministic race from real connected OSM node IDs; the motorcycle and bounded traffic collision cages run on the CPU at 120 Hz.

Startup is a separate CesiumJS north-up hemisphere overview centered on the permitted browser location. Cesium owns the WGS84 globe, camera, bounded AWS Terrarium terrain, contours, OSM overlay, and coarse live-player markers. An official OSM Shortbread z5 window overlays only the regional motorway, trunk, and primary network. That road overlay is intentionally bounded to a center-out 5×5 / 25-tile window rather than pretending to be complete hemisphere coverage. A separate persistent FHWA/U.S. DOT National Highway System layer draws the current versioned nationwide network, including Alaska, from a small same-origin derived MVT snapshot with the original federal metadata retained. Click or tap the Cesium globe to request a seed road region at that point; the overview stays active if no playable OSM road exists. RIDE THIS REGION enters a Three.js/WebXR one-unit-per-metre tangent frame while Cesium, WGS84, and ECEF remain the persistent planetary authority. The terrain working set then advances continuously; Earth-scale coordinates never enter the float/local particle solver.

The ride keeps exact triangle terrain and a six-body particle-rigid motorcycle for collision and dynamics. A deterministic 3×3 active / 5×5 desired Terrarium working set publishes complete collision snapshots only at fixed-step boundaries, retains at most 32 resident chunks, and transforms the local ENU frame without changing the ECEF bike pose. Missing forward terrain pauses substeps as stream-wait; it never resets the bike or invents a flat authoritative surface. Terrarium elevations generate bounded genuine Three.js NURBS contours over a 3.5%-opacity terrain surface. The visible bike is an optimized derivative of “Akira Bike” by Ryanwill679 on Sketchfab, used under CC BY 4.0. The local 7.5 MB GLB is bounded to 58,870 triangles, 15 authored material draws, and 22 KTX2 textures at no more than 1024 px. It is chassis-relative presentation only: the original model is unrigged, so its visible wheels do not articulate, and it never supplies collision or dynamics authority. The six physical bodies, suspension, wheels, tire contacts, and forces remain authoritative. Append ?physicsDebug=1 to expose capped collision particles and contact markers for diagnostics. Full attribution and modification details are packaged in public/models/akira-bike/ATTRIBUTION.md.

The same bounded 5,000 seed-road segments feed rendering and collision. Each road ribbon is clipped against the exact terrain triangles and lifted by one shared 0.105 m deck clearance; its source footprint is packed into a bounded XZ spatial index. A single composite static surface samples the same exact terrain plane plus that clearance inside the footprint and the original terrain everywhere else, avoiding duplicate road/terrain impulses. Physical tire contacts retain the OSM way and segment identifiers. These detailed OSM roads, the race, buildings, and traffic are intentionally seed-region overlays; beyond them the current release is continuous terrain/off-road riding, not a claim of globally streamed street geometry.

A manifest-driven 0.12573690675938365 m presentation-only downward offset puts the calibrated model minimum at the six-body rig's -1.042 m tire-bottom midpoint. This corrects the apparent hover without rescaling the correct 1.67 m wheelbase or changing any physics pose, contact, collision, or force.

The one authored glass material is presented as ordinary alpha transparency at runtime. Its glTF transmission value is disabled after verified loading so Three.js does not redraw the complete 58,870-triangle model for a transmission prepass. Runtime alpha materials also set Three.js forceSinglePass, including the authored double-sided glass, so they cannot fall back to two transparent draws. Race-gate beams use normal frustum culling; neither optimization changes the physics world or the asset's authored opaque materials.

First-, centered-behind damped third-, and stereo-XR camera placement is constrained against the same committed terrain/road snapshot with 0.35 m minimum optical clearance. The sampler, chase state, and XR locomotion root participate in full rotational/translational ENU frame handoffs; XR correction raises only the locomotion root and never overwrites the headset-tracked camera pose. Desktop rendering caps device pixel ratio at 1.5 to bound fragment work on high-density displays; WebXR continues to use the headset compositor's render target.

The map, race course, and traffic are for gameplay and visualization only. They are not navigation, live traffic, road-condition, weather-warning, or terrain-clearance products. Traffic is deterministic application-generated simulation over real road geometry and is always labeled synthetic.

Run it locally

From the repository root, start a bounded geodata backend and the HTTPS Vite app in separate terminals:

# Low-volume development fallback. Production must use a controlled Overpass.
npm run dev:geodata:public
npm run dev:motorcycle

RoadVector normally listens on https://localhost:5188 and proxies /api to the loopback geodata service on port 8788. The current direct Tailnet-only development handoff is:

https://100.68.165.52:5188/

It binds directly to the private Tailnet IP and uses an environment-owned self-signed certificate with that IP in its SAN. It does not use Tailscale Serve, Funnel, or a public tunnel. Accept or install that development certificate on the headset/browser before testing geolocation or immersive WebXR. The same-origin /api proxy keeps the geodata service on loopback.

RoadVector's official Shortbread overview tiles and AWS Terrarium source tiles use a bounded local-only artifact cache owned by PeerCompute StateManager and a second bounded cache at the same-origin geodata backend. The local artifact tier uses memory plus IndexedDB but is separate from the replicated Yjs document, so tile bytes and cache metadata are never sent to peers. Fixed backend routes expose strong ETags and hit/miss/stale state without accepting caller-supplied upstream URLs. A separate session-local 64-entry decoded heightmap LRU coalesces overlapping terrain-halo reads, avoiding repeated PNG/canvas decoding while remaining outside Yjs and below a 16 MiB decoded payload bound. Detailed normalized OSM ride regions retain the backend's existing bounded semantic cache.

Production builds do not silently select a cross-origin backend. Build with VITE_GEODATA_SAME_ORIGIN=true (or VITE_GEODATA_API_URL=same-origin) behind an HTTPS reverse proxy, or set an explicit HTTPS API origin and configure matching GEODATA_CORS_ORIGINS.

Controls

  • Desktop: W/up throttle, A turns left, D turns right, S applies the service brake (front plus rear), space applies front brake only, down applies rear brake only, Q/E manually shift, C changes camera, H recenters, and R resets. The checked AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION control is the default; it uses the physics RPM/hysteresis gearbox, ignores queued manual shift edges, and keeps the bike in a drive gear. Uncheck it to enable Q/E and the touch shift buttons. Mouse steering and pointer lock are off by default so pointer drift cannot bias A/D; an embedding app may explicitly opt into mouse steering, in which case lock loss clears held controls.
  • Mobile: independent Pointer Event steering, throttle, front/rear brake, shift, view, and reset controls support simultaneous touches.
  • Standard gamepad: left axis steering, triggers for throttle/brake, and bounded button mappings through the shared action layer.
  • WebXR controller: xr-standard handed controls, target-ray terminal UI, tracked-head riding, reset, recenter, selection of the non-XR view restored after exit, and session-safe cleanup.
  • Tracked hands: left target-ray yaw establishes a neutral steering pose; select/squeeze provide handed throttle/brake/clutch inputs. Tracking loss neutralizes pose steering. The wrist UI excludes its owning ray, and a one-source session moves the panel to an XR-root fallback so it remains operable.

All devices produce the same immutable normalized ControlFrame; the physics solver never reads browser, Gamepad, or XR objects directly. The UI context captures ride actions only while an interactive spatial control is hovered or pressed.

Simulation and race contract

  • Six particle-cloud rigid components: rider/chassis, rear swingarm, rear wheel, upper fork/handlebars, lower telescoping fork, and front wheel.
  • Deterministic CPU world: fixed 1/120 s, 12 solver iterations, stable IDs, surfaced capacity limits, swept translational and rotational particle CCD, gyroscopic angular integration, hard suspension travel, and snapshot reset.
  • Motorcycle dynamics: engine/reaction torque, separate and service braking, one deterministic filtered actuator state per fixed step, a common curvature/lateral-acceleration target for fork and lean, explicit automatic or manual shifting, steering constraint, suspension springs/dampers, bounded force-based rider-foot parking support, tire slip plus friction ellipse, surface material grip, and optional physical lean-assist torque.
  • Wheel/terrain contact: each wheel retains 32 ring particles for inertia and body/body collision while one solver-owned analytic circular tread shape supplies continuous terrain contact. Its implicit compliant normal impulse and XPBD support use 24,000,000 N/m stiffness and 24,000 N·s/m damping; this removes the rotating polygon-contact phase without smoothing, clamping, or writing the bike pose. The deterministic smooth-road gate sustains at least 26.09 m/s, reaches 30.55 m/s, and measures 0.118 mm chassis-height deviation plus 0.00177 m/s vertical-speed deviation over its final five seconds.
  • Race: a bounded deterministic course derived from the local directed road graph, ordered gates, laps/splits/timer/reset, and bright checkpoint visuals. The forgiving 14 m crossing corridor is independent of the correctly scaled 8 m × 4.2 m visual portal.
  • Traffic: lane-aware, restriction-aware, seeded synthetic vehicles. All 14 default vehicles remain visible; at most four nearby seed-region vehicles have kinematic particle collision cages. Those cages and the race leave the broadphase/progress loop outside the seed region and reactivate on return.
  • World coordinates: all physics uses a local ENU metre frame under a persistent WGS84/ECEF pose. Full frame handoffs rotate and translate body current/previous/predicted state, velocity, gravity, terrain, renderer, camera, and XR anchor in one prepared transaction.

The public window.__motorcycleDemo.getState() surface exposes source provenance, load attempts, terrain degradation, physics/overflow/contact telemetry, wheel loads and slip, suspension travel, traffic/race region state, stream generations/content epochs/residency/coverage, current WGS84/ECEF pose, camera/frame/reset snapshots, and XR panel status for browser gates.

Data, privacy, and limits

  • Exact device coordinates remain in browser memory only; bounded road and terrain source tiles around that coordinate may remain in the disclosed local-only artifact cache. Requests are quantized and expanded to a bounded region before reaching the backend; the service does not log exact query coordinates.
  • Startup requests browser location permission so the opening hemisphere is the viewer's own. Permission denial loads a visibly disclosed Anchorage fallback rather than claiming it is the device location.
  • Real RoadVector peers are shown only in the overview, always in red. Presence publishes at most a coarse 0.1° cell for a permitted browser/manual location, never publishes the automatic fallback, retains no exact coordinates, caps remote markers at 64, and removes entries after 15 seconds. Peer networking failure is nonfatal and never substitutes synthetic traffic as a player.
  • Roads/buildings retain © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, source base time, fetched/freshness/cache state, and derived-database notice.
  • Terrain retains AWS Terrain Tiles registry attribution and not-for-navigation limitations. The Cesium overview is structurally limited to the complete z0–z3 Web Mercator pyramid (85 possible tiles), so moving between locations cannot exhaust a session counter. The initial seed relief retains its bounded deadline and disclosed degraded-plane fallback. Continuous ride terrain uses wrapped z12–z14 keys, a mandatory 3×3 active ring, 5×5 desired prefetch ring, six-request concurrency, 32-resident-chunk LRU, and strict byte/vertex caps. A missing or failed streamed neighbor pauses physics and remains explicitly unavailable; the initial fallback plane is never promoted into global relief.
  • The FHWA National Highway System overview retains the exact upstream version, item/layer identity, archive/member hashes, source counts, original metadata, derivation parameters, tile checksums, and Alaska coverage. It is generalized display geometry and does not imply federal road ownership.
  • Backend density and response caps fail with a smaller-region instruction; the client retries only its fixed bounded radius ladder and never silently truncates a drive graph.

A real headset enter/exit/re-enter, hand/controller, haptics, comfort, and performance pass remains a manual hardware gate. Do not represent the mocked browser gate as physical-headset certification.
