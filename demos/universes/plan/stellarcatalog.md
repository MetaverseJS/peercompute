Here’s a big “taxonomy” of astronomical things you can find in space—from tiny grains up to cosmic-epoch relics. (No list is truly complete, but this covers the major classes and lots of important subtypes.)

1) Fundamental “stuff” and messengers (not really objects, but things you encounter everywhere)

Photons / radiation fields (starlight, infrared glow, UV, X-rays, gamma rays)

Cosmic rays (high-energy protons, nuclei, electrons)

Neutrinos (solar, supernova, cosmic background neutrinos)

Magnetic fields (planetary, stellar, galactic, intergalactic)

Gravitational waves (from compact-object mergers, etc.)

Plasmas (from solar wind to intergalactic plasma)

2) Dust, gas, and “mediums” between objects

Interplanetary dust (zodiacal dust cloud)

Interstellar dust grains (silicates, carbonaceous grains, icy mantles, PAHs)

Gas phases of the interstellar medium (ISM)

Molecular gas (cold H₂; star-forming fuel)

Cold neutral medium

Warm neutral medium

Warm ionized medium

Hot ionized medium / coronal gas

Cloud types

Molecular clouds / Giant molecular clouds

Dark clouds

Diffuse clouds

Bok globules

Circumstellar and interstellar structures

Stellar winds and wind bubbles

Astrospheres (like the heliosphere)

Shock fronts (bow shocks, termination shocks)

Circumgalactic medium (CGM) (gas around galaxies)

Intergalactic medium (IGM) (gas between galaxies)

Intracluster medium (ICM) (hot X-ray gas in galaxy clusters)

3) Small bodies (rocks, ice, leftovers)

Meteoroids / meteors / meteorites

Micrometeorites

Interplanetary debris and collisional families

Asteroids (many ways to classify)

By orbit: Main-belt, Near-Earth objects (NEOs), Atiras/Atens/Apollos/Amors, Mars-crossers

Co-orbitals: Trojans, Greek/ Trojan camps, horseshoe objects

By composition: C-type, S-type, M-type, and lots of sub-classes

By structure: rubble piles, monolithic, binary/ternary asteroids

Comets

By period/origin: Short-period, Long-period, Halley-type, Jupiter-family

Special cases: sungrazers, split comets, dormant/extinct comets

Hybrids: main-belt comets / active asteroids

Interstellar comets (visitors from other systems)

Centaurs (between Jupiter and Neptune-ish populations)

Trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs)

Kuiper Belt objects (KBOs) (classical, resonant like plutinos, etc.)

Scattered disk objects

Detached objects / “Sednoids”

Dwarf planets (a class, plus candidates)

Interstellar objects (e.g., asteroid-like interlopers)

4) Planets and planet types (including exoplanets)

Terrestrial / rocky planets

Super-Earths (bigger rocky worlds)

Sub-Neptunes / mini-Neptunes

Ice giants (Neptune-like)

Gas giants (Jupiter-like)

By temperature/orbit environment:

Hot Jupiters, warm Jupiters

Hot Neptunes

Ultra-short period planets

Temperate / habitable-zone candidates

Rogue planets (free-floating)

By composition hypothesis:

Water worlds / ocean planets

Carbon planets (hypothetical in some formation scenarios)

Iron-rich “super-Mercuries”

Planetary atmospheres (thin, thick, runaway greenhouse, hydrogen-rich envelopes, etc.)

Rings (dense rings, dusty rings, ring arcs)

Magnetospheres (planet-scale plasma environments)

5) Moons and satellite types

Regular moons (formed with the planet, often orderly orbits)

Irregular moons (captured, tilted, eccentric)

Co-orbital moons (sharing orbits)

Shepherd moons (shape rings)

Ocean worlds (subsurface oceans)

Tidally heated moons

Captured asteroids (moonlets)

Exomoons (candidate class around exoplanets)

6) Disks and system-scale structures

Protoplanetary disks (gas+dust disks around young stars)

Debris disks (dust from collisions around mature stars)

Accretion disks

Around young stars

Around white dwarfs / neutron stars / black holes

Around supermassive black holes (AGN disks)

Jets and outflows (protostellar jets, AGN jets)

Tori (dusty structures in active galaxies)

Star systems

Binary, triple, multiple systems

Open clusters, globular clusters

Stellar associations, moving groups

7) Stars (life stages and types)

Formation / early life:

Dense cores, protostars

T Tauri stars, Herbig Ae/Be stars

Main sequence (by spectral class):

O, B, A, F, G, K, M stars

Low-mass and substellar:

Red dwarfs

Brown dwarfs (L, T, Y types)

Evolved stars:

Subgiants

Red giants

Horizontal branch stars

Red clump stars

Asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars

Supergiants / hypergiants

Wolf–Rayet stars

Variable stars (huge family):

Cepheids, RR Lyrae, Miras, eclipsing binaries, flare stars, etc.

8) Stellar remnants and compact objects

White dwarfs (including pulsating WDs; WD binaries)

Neutron stars

Pulsars

Magnetars

X-ray binaries (compact object accreting from companion)

Black holes

Stellar-mass black holes

Intermediate-mass black holes (candidate class)

Supermassive black holes (galactic centers)

Compact-object systems

NS–NS, BH–BH, NS–BH binaries (gravitational-wave sources)

Pulsar wind nebulae (object + surrounding nebula)

9) Nebulae and “cloud objects”

Emission nebulae (H II regions around hot young stars)

Reflection nebulae

Dark nebulae

Planetary nebulae (shells from dying Sun-like stars)

Protoplanetary nebulae (transition stage before planetary nebula)

Supernova remnants (expanding shock shells + filaments)

Herbig–Haro objects (knots where jets hit gas)

Wolf–Rayet nebulae (wind-blown bubbles)

Molecular cloud cores (star-forming clumps)

10) Galaxies and active galactic nuclei (AGN)

Galaxy morphologies:

Spiral (grand design, flocculent)

Barred spiral

Lenticular (S0)

Elliptical

Irregular

By scale / brightness:

Dwarf galaxies (dSph, dIrr, etc.)

Ultra-diffuse galaxies

By activity:

Starburst galaxies

Active galaxies / AGN

Seyfert galaxies

Quasars

Blazars

Radio galaxies

Substructures and components:

Bulge, disk, halo, stellar streams

Dark matter halos (inferred structures)

Giant molecular cloud complexes

Galactic bars, spiral arms

11) Groups, clusters, and large-scale structure

Galaxy groups

Galaxy clusters

Superclusters (sometimes loosely defined)

Cosmic web

Filaments

Sheets / walls

Voids

Intracluster light (stars not bound to any one galaxy)

12) Cosmological relics and early-universe “historical object types”

Primordial plasma (early hot ionized universe; includes eras where matter existed as plasma)

Even earlier: quark–gluon plasma (very early-universe state)

Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) (relic radiation)

Cosmic neutrino background (relic neutrinos; not directly imaged yet)

Reionization structures (ionized “bubbles” around first galaxies/quasars)

Primordial element abundances (light nuclei from big-bang nucleosynthesis)

Hypothetical/uncertain relic objects (often discussed, not confirmed):

Primordial black holes

Cosmic strings / topological defects

13) (Optional) Human-made “objects in space”

Satellites, space stations

Space probes

Rocket bodies

Space debris (fragment clouds, paint flecks, etc.)
